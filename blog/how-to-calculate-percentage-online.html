How to Calculate Percentage Increase, Decrease & Discounts

A checkout discount, a pay rise, an exam grade, a restaurant tip — percentages show up constantly in ordinary life, and the mental math is easy to get wrong under pressure. The formulas themselves are simple once you see them written out plainly, and a calculator removes the arithmetic entirely.

The three percentage calculations people need most

Percentage of a number — "what is 20% of 85?" — multiply the number by the percentage divided by 100. What percentage one number is of another — "45 is what % of 60?" — divide the part by the whole and multiply by 100. Percentage change — "a price went from 80 to 100, what's the increase?" — subtract old from new, divide by the old number, then multiply by 100. Nearly every real-world percentage question is one of these three, phrased differently.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the formula for a percentage discount?

Multiply the original price by the discount percentage, then subtract that from the original price. A $60 item at 25% off: 60 × 0.25 = 15, then 60 − 15 = $45. The same calculation works for tips and sales tax, just added instead of subtracted.

How do I calculate a percentage increase between two numbers?

Subtract the old number from the new number, divide that by the old number, then multiply by 100. Going from 80 to 100 is (100 − 80) ÷ 80 × 100, which equals a 25% increase.

Why isn't a percentage increase the same as the decrease in reverse?

Because the base number changes. A 50% increase from 100 reaches 150, but a 50% decrease from 150 only returns to 75, not 100. Percentage change is always relative to its own starting point, which is why the two directions aren't symmetrical — a detail Percentage Calculator handles automatically so you don't have to think about it.

How do I calculate what percentage one number is of another?

Divide the part by the whole, then multiply by 100. 45 out of 60 is 45 ÷ 60 × 100, which equals 75%.
