Add Images to PDF

Insert photos or scanned pages into a PDF you already have. Choose the PDF, add your JPG, PNG or WebP images, and each one is added as a new page - at the end of the document or after any page you choose. The whole job runs in your browser, so neither the PDF nor the images are uploaded.

Step 1 — the PDF you are adding to

Click to choose a PDF, or drag and drop One PDF. Its existing pages are left exactly as they are.

Step 2 — the images to insert

Click to choose images, or drag and drop JPG, PNG or WebP. Each image becomes one new page, in the order shown below.

Add Images to a PDF Online, Free

Sometimes a PDF needs one more page rather than a whole new document - a photo of a signed form stapled to a contract, a receipt scanned after the invoice, a diagram that belongs in the middle of a report. This tool opens the PDF you already have and adds your images to it as additional pages, in the position you pick. The original pages are untouched: nothing is re-rendered, re-typed or compressed, so the document you get back is the one you started with plus your pictures.

How to add images to a PDF

  1. Choose the PDF you want to add to.
  2. Add the images - JPG, PNG or WebP. Each one becomes a new page, in the order shown.
  3. Choose whether the images go at the end of the PDF or after a page number you type in.
  4. Pick the page size: match the existing PDF page and fit the image inside it, or size each new page to its own image.
  5. Click Add images & download PDF. The updated document downloads straight away.

At the end, or after any page

Append the images to the document, or slot them in after page 3, page 12 - wherever they belong.

JPG, PNG and WebP

Drop in as many images as you need. Photos, screenshots and scans all work; each becomes its own page.

Nothing re-encoded

JPEG and PNG images are embedded as they are, so the quality of your originals survives the trip.

Matching the page or the image

Match the PDF page is usually the right choice for a document: the new page takes its width and height from the PDF you are adding to, and the image is scaled down to sit inside it with a comfortable margin. The result looks like a normal page of the same document. Size each page to its own image instead makes the new page exactly the size of the picture, which is handy for photos and scans that shouldn't have white borders - but it means the new pages may be a different size from the rest of the PDF.

Privacy

Your PDF and your images are opened, combined and saved entirely inside your browser tab. Nothing is sent to EasyWorkTools.online or any third party, and no copy of either file is stored anywhere but on your own device.

Limitations

Images are added as whole pages - this tool does not place a picture into a corner of an existing page (use Add Text to PDF or your PDF editor for overlaid content). HEIC photos straight from an iPhone cannot be read by the browser, so convert them first with HEIC to JPG. Password-protected PDFs must be unlocked before they can be opened, and very large scans will take a moment to embed.

Add Images to PDF FAQ

Can I add images to a PDF without an account?

Yes. There is no sign-up, no email and no watermark. Choose the PDF and the images, click the button and the updated document downloads.

Can I insert an image in the middle of a PDF?

Yes. Choose "After a page number" and type the page the images should follow. They are inserted immediately after that page, in the order you added them.

Which image formats can I use?

JPG and JPEG, PNG and WebP. A WebP image is converted to PNG so that it can be embedded. HEIC files from an iPhone are not supported by the browser - convert them to JPG first.

Are my images compressed or resized?

JPEG and PNG images are embedded exactly as they are; only the on-page size is changed, never the stored image data. WebP images are converted to PNG, which can make that page larger.

Can I add several images at once?

Yes. Add as many as you like and each one becomes its own new page, in the order they appear in the list.

Is my PDF uploaded anywhere?

No. Both the PDF and the images are processed in your browser using a local library, so nothing leaves your device.
