Content Summarizer

Content Summarizer

How this works: this is a private, browser-based text tool. Your text is processed on this page and never uploaded, logged or sent to a third party. It applies measurable rules to your writing and shows you what it changed — it is not a hosted AI model, and it cannot judge whether your content is true.

What this tool does

The summary is extractive, not generative. It selects the sentences that carry your key phrases and keeps them in their original wording, in their original order. Nothing is paraphrased and nothing is invented.

If the lead sentence of your article is a good summary already, that is often exactly what comes back - and that is not a bug, it is a signal about your first paragraph.

How to use it

  1. Paste the text you want summarised.
  2. Choose the summary length.
  3. Press Summarise text.
  4. Check the percentage reduction at the bottom.
  5. Edit the summary so it reflects what you actually think the piece says.

Tip: press Ctrl + Enter (or Cmd + Enter on a Mac) to run the tool without leaving the text box.

Where it helps

  • Summarising a long article for notes or a newsletter.
  • Getting the gist of a report before reading it fully.
  • Producing an abstract for a document you wrote.
  • Condensing research before sharing it internally.

Frequently asked questions

Does it rewrite the summary in its own words?

No. It is extractive: it picks the original sentences and keeps their exact wording. Nothing is rephrased, so nothing can be misrepresented.

Why does my summary start with the first sentence?

It ranks sentences by how many of your key phrases they contain, and breaks ties by position. The lead sentence usually wins because it is usually the summary.

Which length should I use?

3 sentences for a headline summary, 5 for a paragraph, one paragraph for a research abstract, and "Key points only" when you want a bulleted list.

Can I use it on very short text?

You can, but there is little to choose from - a two-sentence input will come back nearly intact. It works best on a few paragraphs or more.
